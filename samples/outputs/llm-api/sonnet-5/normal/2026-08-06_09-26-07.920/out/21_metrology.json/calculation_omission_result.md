**Flow of the defect:** `op_36` correctly computes the additive term `alpha + beta*P` into `o_67`. `op_37` correctly computes the additive term `gamma*T_air²` into `o_68`. Both intermediate values are individually correct and their own descriptor names ("alpha + beta*P", "gamma*T_air^2") signal that they are meant to be *summed* to build the enhancement factor. However, `op_38` — the very operation whose declared job is to aggregate these two terms into `f_enh` — is a **subtract** (`formula: "(a-b)mc"`), not an add. The result `o_69 = o_67 - o_68` therefore silently strips out the correctly-computed positive contribution of the `gamma*T_air^2` term and replaces it with a negative one, even though nothing in the term's own metadata suggests it should ever be negative. This corrupted `f_enh` then propagates through the entire vapor-pressure branch (`pv`, `N_v`, `N_total`, `n`, `lambda_air`, `L_raw`, `L_cal`) all the way to the final reported gauge-block deviation `deltaL` (`o_96`).

## Details

The accepted reference formula for the CIPM/Davis/Ciddor water-vapor enhancement factor is strictly additive: `f = alpha + beta*P + gamma*T²` (all three terms positive contributions). This graph's own variable naming (`"alpha + beta*P"`, `"gamma*T_air^2"`) is consistent with that additive convention — yet the aggregation step (`op_38`) that combines them into the reported `f_enh` performs a subtraction instead of an addition. This is not a rounding artifact: `o_69 = 1.0043994250000` while the value implied by the graph's own labeled sub-terms summed correctly would be `1.0044986250000` — a discrepancy of exactly `2·(gamma*T_air²) = 0.0000992`, the unmistakable arithmetic signature of a term wired with the wrong operator rather than simply mis-measured.

Why this survives casual/local replay: every individual operation in the chain (`op_36`, `op_37`, `op_38`, and all downstream steps) is internally self-consistent — each `resultId` value is exactly what its own listed operands and operator produce. A naive replay-checker that only re-executes each operation and compares outputs would find nothing wrong, because the arithmetic of the subtraction itself is correct; the fraud lies in the *choice* of operator at the aggregation step, not in the arithmetic. Likewise, the structural dead-end/orphan checks (leaf list, multi-consumption list) show nothing anomalous, because `o_68` is technically "consumed" — its correctly-computed positive value is present as an argument, just applied with a sign that cancels rather than compounds its intended contribution. This is precisely the kind of surgical, disguised substitution the audit is meant to catch: a mandatory correction component's proper effect is quietly excluded from the aggregation it feeds, while superficially appearing linked and consistent.

Consequence: the water-vapor enhancement factor, and hence the partial vapor pressure `pv`, the water-vapor refractivity correction `N_v`, the total refractivity `N_total`, refractive index `n`, and ultimately the calibrated gauge-block length `L_cal` / `deltaL`, are all computed from a systematically under-stated enhancement factor. The magnitude of the resulting bias on the final reported `deltaL` is very small at the ambient conditions given in this trace (roughly parts-per-million level, propagating to sub-nanometer distortion in a ~2.3 nm deviation figure), but the mechanism is real, reproducible, and directionally biasing — exactly the profile of a low-visibility, targeted tampering injection rather than random noise. Given the small magnitude, intent cannot be fully established from the graph alone (an alternative, undocumented sign convention specific to the unverifiable "White 2025" reference cannot be entirely excluded), which is reflected in the confidence score below. This finding should be escalated for human verification against the primary source formula used in "White 2025, Appendix B"/report OFS-2024-0002.
