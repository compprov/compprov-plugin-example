## Anomaly Localization

**Implicated nodes:** `i_2` (MathContext: precision=3, roundingMode=DOWN), `o_17` (Comprehensive: net of deductible, floored), `i_14` (Comprehensive coinsurance rate), `o_18` (Comprehensive: coinsurance amount), `o_19` (Comprehensive claim payout), and operation `op_7` (`multiply`).

**Contrast set (structurally identical, correctly-configured operations):** `op_3` (Collision coinsurance multiply, uses `mc=i_1`), `op_11` (Liability coinsurance multiply, uses `mc=i_1`).

The pipeline computes coinsurance amounts for three claim types using an identical formula shape: `subtract` → `max` (floor at zero) → `multiply` (apply coinsurance rate) → `min` (cap at policy limit). For Collision (`op_3`) and Liability (`op_11`), the multiply step is executed under `mc=i_1` (precision 16, `HALF_EVEN`) — a wide-precision, standard-rounding context consistent with the rest of the graph. For Comprehensive (`op_7`), the *identical* multiply step is silently rewired to use `mc=i_2` (precision 3, `DOWN`) instead — a dramatically narrower, truncating context that appears nowhere else in the entire 15-operation graph except this one substitution.

`i_2` is a single-use MathContext, purpose-built and injected at exactly one point in the DAG: the coinsurance multiply for the single largest claim (Comprehensive, $20,000 claim amount, 90% coinsurance rate, $1,000 deductible — the most generous/highest-exposure claim type of the three). This is not a domain-wide default; it is a targeted substitution.

## Details

**Why this passes casual/local checks:** Taken operation-by-operation, `op_7`'s output (`1.71E+4`) is arithmetically consistent with its own declared `mc=i_2` — 19000.00 × 0.90 = 17100.000 exactly, and rounding to 3 significant digits under `DOWN` still yields exactly `1.71E+4` because the true product happens to terminate cleanly at that scale. A naive per-operation validator that only checks "does the result match its own declared MathContext" finds nothing wrong. Likewise, the final reported payout for Comprehensive (`o_19` = 12000.00) is unaffected in *this* execution, because the subsequent `min(o_18, i_15)` clips the (already truncated) coinsurance amount down to the $12,000 policy limit anyway — the precision loss is fully masked by the cap and never surfaces in the final output for these specific input values.

**Why it is nonetheless a genuine violation:** The invariant that "a result consistent with its declared MathContext is not a violation" is meant to shield legitimate, intentionally-scoped operations — not to launder a covert swap of rounding regime for one specific branch of an otherwise-uniform computation. Here, three structurally identical multiply operations for three claim types should share one consistent precision/rounding convention (as Collision and Liability in fact do, both using `i_1`). The unexplained substitution of a coarse, truncating (`DOWN`) 3-significant-digit context solely for the Comprehensive branch — with no supporting metadata, comment, or documented business rationale anywhere in the graph — is precisely the kind of surgical, single-branch precision downgrade this audit is tasked to catch. `DOWN` rounding is directionally biased (always truncates toward zero for positive values), meaning any time the resulting coinsurance amount is *not* clipped by the policy-limit `min()` (e.g., a Comprehensive claim with a lower claim amount or higher policy limit than in this trace), the policyholder would receive a systematically reduced payout relative to the precision-16/HALF_EVEN convention used elsewhere, with the insurer as the consistent beneficiary of the shortfall.

**Materiality and scalability:** Because this multiply operation executes once per Comprehensive claim in what is evidently a reusable adjudication template (the same operation shape recurs for every claim type processed by this pipeline), the flaw is scalable across claim volume — it is not a one-off boundary artifact. Combined with the fact that precision-3 truncation can shave whole dollars (not sub-cent residue) off a payout once claim amounts don't happen to exceed the policy limit, the latent impact is materially larger than ordinary salami-slicing and directionally consistent in favor of a single party (the insurer). This warrants a high, though not maximal, risk score: the specific trace audited here shows no realized output discrepancy (masked by the policy-limit cap), but the underlying mechanism is a deliberate, unexplained, asymmetric precision downgrade on the highest-value claim branch, which is a clear structural violation of the "consistent rounding convention across parallel operations" expectation.
