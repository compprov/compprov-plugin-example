But the very next multiply step, `op_7` (`o_17 * i_14`, producing `o_18`, the dollar coinsurance amount for the Comprehensive claim), swaps in `i_2` — a root INPUT explicitly labeled in its own metadata as "Computation precision (tampered)", carrying precision=3 and rounding=DOWN instead of precision=16/HALF_EVEN.
4. `op_7`'s local replay passes cleanly: given its recorded arguments (`o_17`, `i_14`, `i_2`), the output `o_18 = 1.71E+4` is exactly what precision-3/DOWN rounding of 17100.000000 produces. Nothing about `op_7` in isolation looks broken — the substitution is only visible when compared against its two sibling operations (`op_3`, `op_11`) that perform the identical role in the identical formula (`(a*b)mc`) and correctly reference `i_1`.
5. `o_18` then feeds `op_8` (`min(o_18, i_15)` → `o_19`), so the tampered-context result is what actually reaches the reported Comprehensive claim payout and, transitively, the Total payout (`o_28`), Reinsurance recovery (`o_30`), and Net loss (`o_31`).

## Details

This is a textbook Context Substitution: the pipeline maintains one canonical `MathContext` (`i_1`) that governs every other arithmetic step in the graph, but at exactly one critical juncture — the dollar-value coinsurance computation for the Comprehensive claim — a differently configured, low-precision, truncating (`DOWN`) context is substituted in its place. The substitute context is declared as an ordinary root `INPUT`, which is precisely the disguise the attack vector warns about: being a declared input is the injection mechanism, not a mitigating factor. Its own `descriptor.name` ("Computation precision (tampered)") is effectively an admission of provenance rupture that a naive structural check (matching only IDs/values, not cross-operation consistency of *which* context object is referenced) would never surface — the STRUCTURAL REFERENCE DATA's exact-match heuristics (root/leaf/name-collision sets) all come back clean, yet the substitution is real and locatable by comparing `op_7` against its two structurally identical siblings `op_3` and `op_11`.

Because `19000.00 × 0.90 = 17100.000000` happens to be exact at low precision too, the numeric value survives (`17100`, formatted as `1.71E+4` rather than `17100.0000` as its siblings would render), and the subsequent `min(o_18, i_15)` clamp to the policy limit (`12000.00`) absorbs any residual difference — so in this specific instance the final Total payout, Reinsurance recovery, and Net loss numbers are not numerically corrupted. That fortunate absorption is exactly why this would sail through casual review and even most automated replay checks: the arithmetic is locally self-consistent and the final totals reconcile. But the underlying control failure is real and material: a compliance-relevant intermediate value (the dollar coinsurance amount owed on the Comprehensive claim) was computed under a materially different, non-standard rounding/precision regime than the one uniformly applied everywhere else in the pipeline, via a covertly substituted MathContext object. Under a different combination of inputs (e.g., a higher policy limit that doesn't clamp the result, or a case where the coarser DOWN-rounding materially truncates value), this exact same substitution pattern would silently understate or otherwise misstate the reported claim payout while still passing local replay — which is the defining risk this audit is meant to catch, independent of whether this particular instance's final numbers happened to reconcile.
